THE MECHANISM, read from pcrec's own design note (NOT guessed): `git -C
~/pcrec show d6cb0bb4f3:docs/design/hyb_reseed.md` section 1 ("The
defect") and section 4's row table (read-only; d6cb0bb4f3 is the
[OPT-HYB-RESEED] landing merge). On a clamp-free hybrid (rows `exact`/
`fixed`), the OLD behaviour is: "When the VM attempt [at the
prefilter's candidate] fails, the loop advances one character. It asks
the prefilter again ONLY when an MRL clamp exists... So on a clamp-free
hybrid, one failed attempt makes the loop STEP EVERY CHARACTER BOUNDARY
TO THE SUBJECT END, running a VM attempt at each." The row table's own
words for `exact`/`fixed`: "today's retry, unchanged: the clamp
recompute where a clamp exists, ELSE STEP." So `denied`
(`-fno-hyb-reseed`, "today's retry") does NOT re-seed on these three
clamp-free patterns -- it STEPS, one VM attempt per byte, for the
REMAINDER of the subject after the first failed attempt. The real fix
(`default`, rows `adaptive`/`adaptive-dense`) is what RE-SEEDS -- it
re-asks the DFA prefilter periodically to skip past non-candidate
stretches instead of attempting every byte. This explains the
magnitude directly: on a long subject, "step every byte to the end
once a candidate fails" is O(remaining subject length) VM attempts,
which is exactly why the ratio GROWS with subject size on the ordinary
throughput subjects (t-64k -> t-256k -> t-1m) while staying near the
predicted band on I-114's short, density-tuned synthetic ones.

ITEM 1 predictions:

1a. "varwidth and neg on any subject with sparse candidates are x2-x21
    faster" -- CONFIRMED IN DIRECTION, MAGNITUDE FAR EXCEEDED on the
    real (ordinary) corpus. I-114's own synthetic subjects
    (synth-64k-asc, synth-1m) land roughly IN the predicted x2-x21
    band on varwidth (1.17-3.95x). But on `bench/utf8`'s own REAL
    throughput prose (t-64k/t-256k/t-1m/t-64k-asc/t-64k-lat) the
    speedup is 11x-99x, an order of magnitude past the predicted
    ceiling -- SEE THE MECHANISM ABOVE: once the first candidate
    fails, the denied arm steps every remaining byte of the subject
    running a VM attempt at each, so the cost scales with how much of
    the subject is LEFT after the first failure, which is far larger
    on a 1 MB ordinary-prose subject than on I-114's own short,
    density-tuned ones. **This is the headline finding**: the real
    fix's win on ordinary text is much larger than I-114's own Mac
    scratch measurement predicted, not merely in the predicted range.
