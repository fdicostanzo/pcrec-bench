1. THE DECISION RULE FIRES BRANCH A: the ~x1.08 does NOT reproduce.
   cf0962e3 median 6,303.58 ns/call, 25b1984f median 6,304.74 ns/call --
   a difference of 1.16 ns (0.02%), against report.py's own R8 cross-pin
   rule (`unchanged (within spread)`: medians differ by no more than 2x
   the larger stddev). Excluding trial 4's outlier (below), the medians
   are 6,301.69 ns and 6,304.74 ns -- a 3.05 ns / 0.05% difference,
   ratio 1.0005. Both readings say the same thing: on this box, in this
   session, through the SAME wrapper and toolchain, the two pins are
   indistinguishable on this cell. Per the agreed decision rule, this
   closes O-38's mover as a between-session/box effect -- NOTHING is
   filed as a pcrec item.

2. ONE OUTLIER, named and explained rather than absorbed: trial 4 on
   cf0962e3 read 17,552.56 ns/call, ~2.8x every other trial on either
   pin (range otherwise 6,276.71-6,499.77 ns). This inflates
   cf0962e3's naive stddev to 3,243.29 ns (51.452%) -- a single-trial
   spike, not a systematic shift: the MEDIAN (robust to one outlier by
   construction) reads 6,303.58 ns either way, and removing the outlier
   entirely changes the cross-pin verdict's magnitude by under 2 ns.
   Consistent with ordinary box noise (a scheduling hiccup, a page
   fault storm on that one process) rather than anything pin-related --
   it fired on cf0962e3's 4th interleaved launch, with 25b1984f's own
   4th launch (measured in the SAME few seconds) reading a perfectly
   ordinary 6,304.20 ns. No `/proc/stat` timeline was captured for this
   scratch-tier run (the v1.4 provenance timeline is a PINNED-record
   facility); the outlier is reported as an observation, not diagnosed
   further.

3. THE BUFFER-PLACEMENT FINDING (STEP 3): `frames_addr % 64` and
   `trail_addr % 64` are BOTH EXACTLY 16 on EVERY ONE of the 24 trials,
   both regions, both pins -- with no exception. The addresses' higher
   bits visibly vary launch to launch (ASLR is active: e.g. cf0962e3's
   frames pointers range from 0x71ba... to 0x7fee... across the 12
   trials), but the low 6 bits -- the byte offset within a 64-byte cache
   line -- are PINNED, not random. This is the opposite of I-79's own
   working hypothesis ("an 8% move on a ~6 us cell from a frame array
   crossing a cache-line or page boundary is the expected magnitude" --
   inbox I-79 (ii).3, implicitly assuming ASLR varies cache-line
   placement launch to launch). It does not: on this box, for this
   allocation shape (`posix_memalign` via `alloc_region()`, sized
   32768 x resume_frame_size / 131072 x trail_frame_size bytes), the
   cache-line offset is DETERMINISTIC. The mechanism is not established
   here (this probe does not read `pb_buffer_align()`'s compile-time
   value or glibc's allocator internals), but the observation itself is
   unambiguous over 24 independent process launches: buffer placement
   relative to a 64-byte line is not a source of run-to-run variance on
   this box for this artifact, which independently supports branch A's
   reading above (there is no varying mechanism here that a
   between-session comparison could have caught crossing differently).

4. WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE. The historical two-window comparison
   (2026-09-18 vs 2026-09-20/21, separate `pcrecbench run` invocations
   days apart under the full quiet-gate window discipline) still shows
   +509.14 ns / x1.08 in the committed store records; this same-session
   probe cannot re-examine THOSE two specific processes' own buffer
   placement (they no longer exist), only whether the effect
   reproduces under a fresh, controlled, interleaved comparison -- it
   does not, and finding 3 above removes buffer cache-line placement as
   a plausible cross-session mechanism. The residual explanation is an
   ordinary between-session box/environment effect (kernel scheduling
   state, ASLR's higher-order placement interacting with something else
   entirely, thermal/frequency scaling state two days apart, or ordinary
   measurement noise at ~91x the spread-rule threshold on a single
   isolated cell out of a much larger AFTER sample) -- outside what a
   same-session witness can distinguish further, and outside this
   lane's charter to pursue past the agreed decision rule.
