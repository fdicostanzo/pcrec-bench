## 1(c). The two all-timed-out groups

Which: for **pcre2-interp, pcre2-interp-deep and onig-default-deep**, the group (`cscan`, throughput) and
(`cscan-onig`, throughput) on `nest-brace-100k` (one subject each; "2 all-timed-out" in each record's agreement line;
5 trials each, all `timed-out`, diagnostic "the per-subject alarm fired"). The same two cells on onig-default (non-deep) are
WRONG (instant no-match), on pcre2-jit are `-46`, on pcre2-jit-deep and pcrec `_in` they match.

Cap or pathology: **both** -- a pathology that the 60 s per-subject cap converts into a timeout. The ladder is
quadratic: pcre2-interp 8k 1.64 s -> 10k 2.54 s -> 32k 31.0 s (exponent ~2.1 between 10k and 32k), so 100k extrapolates to
~320 s, 5x the cap; onig-deep 8k 0.56 s -> 32k 9.2 s (x16.4 for x4 the size), 100k extrapolates to ~86 s, 1.4x the cap.
pcre2-jit-deep (772 us at 32k) and pcrec `_in` (3.2 ms) are linear. So the interp timeout is a pathology (the NOTES.md
section 3 finding about libpcre2 10.46 -- "a nesting depth the matcher supports costs super-quadratic time" -- now
reproduced by the harness), and the Onig one is a marginal cap hit. Cost to the cell: 2 patterns x 5 trials x 60 s = 600 s
of timeouts per such cell. Not read: what the cell would take with an unlimited cap (not run).
