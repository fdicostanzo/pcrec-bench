## 1. Method note: what "the same cell" means in this report

The set-grain report's `rank` section carries one row per (pattern,
regime, testee, metric) with `subject_or_na == "(set)"` — the median
AGGREGATED over every subject in that regime (91 short subjects for
`short-subject-search`, all 7 throughput subjects for
`large-subject-throughput`). This is the CELL the ranking rule's own
intro defines. R2 and the R5 completion read this row; R3/R4/R7 read the
subject-grain slice's per-subject rows instead, because their own text
names specific subjects (`t-64k-asc`, `t-1m`, `t-64k-lat`/`-cyr`/`-cjk`).

## 3. R2 — the pcre2-jit band

> **R2 — the `pcre2-jit` band** (Frank's own, I-42 (3)): a cell against
> `pcre2-utf-jit`'s same cell, worse than ×2 or better than ×20, in
> either direction.

Operationalized as: `ratio = cell_ns / jit_ns` for every (pattern,
regime, testee≠jit) cell present on both sides; a hit is `ratio > 2`
("worse than ×2") or `ratio < 1/20` ("better than ×20"). Extract lines
6-21 (population + per-testee split), 45-60 (worst 15).

**431 of 1,340 comparable cells (32.2%) trip R2.** Per testee (extract
lines 12-21):

| testee | slower (>×2) | faster (>×20) | comparable |
|---|---|---|---|
| `libpcre2-dfa` | 53 | 0 | 149 |
| `libpcre2-interp` | 55 | 0 | 150 |
| `oniguruma` | 64 | 4 | 121 |
| `pcrec-auto-caps` | 3 | 9 | 138 |
| `pcrec-auto-nocaps` | 3 | 9 | 138 |
| `pcrec-vm-caps` | 24 | 7 | 134 |
| `pcrec-vm-in-caps` | 24 | 7 | 134 |
| `re2-utf8` | 70 | 8 | 124 |
| `rust-default` | 11 | 20 | 116 |
| `vectorscan-nosom` | 0 | 60 | 136 |

**Verdict: R2 is NOT a small population here — it is close to a
one-in-three census, the largest hit rate of the six rules scored in
this addendum.** Reading the per-(testee,regime) split (extract lines
23-43) narrows it: every non-jit-comparable "slower" cell for
`libpcre2-dfa`/`-interp`/`oniguruma`/`re2` sits at ordinary
interpreter-vs-JIT magnitude (a few×) on BOTH regimes — unsurprising,
since JIT is the fastest engine by construction and these are the four
non-JIT'd engines. `vectorscan-nosom`'s 60/69 throughput cells are ALL
"better than ×20" and NONE of its 67 short-search cells trip either
threshold; `rust-default` similarly has 20/59 throughput "faster" hits
and only 1 "slower". Both point at the SAME already-documented
mechanism: `vectorscan`'s `nosom` boolean-grain fast path (per
`testees/vectorscan/CLAUDE.md`) reports match/no-match without full
span work, which can leave a throughput cell disproportionately cheap
regardless of subject size — R7 below shows the same population moving
opposite the expected direction on the same subjects. pcrec's own
"faster" population (`auto` 9/138, `vm` 7/134, all on `large-subject-
throughput`) is its compiled-DFA-vs-JIT win, already the ledger's known
story; its own "slower" population is concentrated on the forced-`vm`
route (24 vs `auto`'s 3) — extract lines not reproduced in full here
(see the archive) show the worst pcrec-vm cells are the six `prp-*`
patterns (`prp-lu` ×1,189, `prp-n` ×140, `prp-han` ×72, …) — the same
family (f) population R5/P7 already name as pcrec's known property-
family cost, now read against jit rather than against pcrec's own
median.

**Ranking discipline note (NOTES.md's own "first rule it trips" /
"algorithmically-general first" ordering):** per R0/R1 cross-checks (§5
below), none of the 431 R2 hits sit in the wrong-answer or refusal
population — R2 is reading real timing cells throughout.

**What was NOT read:** no cause is asserted beyond stamp-independent
timing (R6, below, cannot be scored from this report at all — see §6);
whether any INDIVIDUAL R2 cell (as opposed to the vectorscan/rust
census-shaped population) is itself "a cell whose likely fix is a

the SAME "faster than ×20" population, not a distinct worse case.

## 4. R7 — sweep flatness

> **R7 — a non-flat sweep.** `t-1m` ns/byte against `t-64k` ns/byte
> outside [0.7, 1.4] on one pattern and testee, read on EVERY pattern.

`ns/byte = median_ns / subject_bytes` (`t-1m` = 1,048,576 B, `t-64k` =
65,536 B, `bench/utf8/subject_facts.tsv`). Extract lines 63-158.

**91 of 750 (pattern, testee) pairs (12.1%) trip R7.** No pair is
missing both subjects (750 = 75 patterns × 10 testees with both rows;
pcrec's `emit_bytes`-cliff-adjacent refusals are the same 5 rows R1
already reads, not a subject-availability gap here). The population by
direction:

- **`vectorscan-nosom` is 45 of the 91 hits, and every one of them is
  the LOW-ratio direction** (`t-1m` cheaper per byte than `t-64k`,
  ratios 0.009-0.28) — e.g. `lit-nfc-pair` ratio 0.0093 (t1m 0.000088
  ns/B vs t64k 0.009391 ns/B, extract line 70). This is the SAME
  mechanism R2's census flagged: the `nosom` boolean-grain fast path can
  answer disproportionately fast once a match is found early, and a 1 MB
  subject with an early hit costs no more in absolute time than a 64 KB
  one with the same early hit, which collapses ns/byte as the
  denominator grows. Two `pcrec-vm-*` cells and two `re2`/`onig` cells
  sit in the same low-ratio band at far milder ratios (0.04-0.06).
- **The HIGH-ratio direction (`t-1m` costing MORE per byte) is smaller
  and pcrec-concentrated**: `lit-1ch-4b` on `pcrec-auto-{caps,nocaps}`
  (ratio 2.45, extract lines 68-69) and `pcrec-vm-{caps,in}` (ratio
  ~1.95-1.96, lines 80/88) is the single worst high-side member on every
  pcrec config that measures it; `asr-dollar-ml`, `lit-offset-at-tail`,
  `lit-mixed-ascii`, `lit-nfc-pair`, `alt-shared-char`, `cls-s-nbsp` each
  cross 1.4 on one or more pcrec configs at more modest ratios (1.4-1.9).

**Verdict:** R7 fires on a real, if modest, population — 1 in 8 pairs —
concentrated in two SEPARATE, already-partially-known mechanisms: the
vectorscan `nosom` fast-path artifact (low side, already named in
`testees/vectorscan/CLAUDE.md`) and a smaller pcrec high-side population
whose worst member, `lit-1ch-4b` (the one 4-byte-character literal,
`😀`), is not otherwise flagged by R2/R3/R4/R5 in this addendum — a
CANDIDATE finding (§7 below).

**What was NOT read:** the direction split above is read off the raw
numbers; no cause is asserted for `lit-1ch-4b`'s specific mechanism (no
mechanism stamp is available — see R6, §6). `t-256k` (the sweep's middle
rung) is not part of R7's own comparison and was not read here.

## 5. R3 — the encoding band

> **R3 — the ENCODING band, this set's own rule.** A cell whose
> `median_ns` differs from the SAME pattern's same cell on the `asc`
> subject population by more than ×3, where the pattern is PURE ASCII
> (the floor, `ci-ascii-control`, `asr-b-ascii`).

Read at `large-subject-throughput` subject grain, the three named
patterns only, against `t-64k-asc`, over `t-64k-lat`/`-cyr`/`-cjk`.
Extract lines 163-187.

**22 of 99 (pattern, testee, script) triples (22.2%) trip R3** — all 11
testees are present for all 3 patterns against `t-64k-asc` (33 of 33
pairs; no NA). The 22 hits split cleanly by pattern:

- **`asr-b-ascii`** (10 of the 22): pcrec's four configs on
  `t-64k-cyr`/`-cjk` (ratio 0.0042-0.0306) and `vm`/`vm-in` additionally
  on `t-64k-lat` (ratio 0.058); `vectorscan-nosom` on `-cyr`/`-cjk`
  (ratio 0.268); `rust-default` on `-cyr`/`-cjk` (ratio 0.318-0.319). In
  every one of these ten the pattern is CHEAPER per byte on the non-ASCII
  script than on ASCII — the direction R3 calls out as "the subject's
  byte structure reaching a mechanism that should not have noticed".
- **`ci-ascii-control`** (6 of the 22): `oniguruma`, both `libpcre2`
  routes present (`dfa`, `interp`), and pcrec's `auto`/`auto-nocaps` — all
  on `t-64k-cjk` except one (`pcrec-*` on `t-64k-cyr`); all in the
  CHEAPER-on-non-ASCII direction (ratio 0.27-0.32).
- **`floor`** (3 of the 22): `libpcre2-dfa`/`-interp`/`-jit`, all on
  `t-64k-cyr` only, and all in the OPPOSITE direction — MORE expensive
  per byte on Cyrillic than on ASCII (ratio 3.05-3.12, e.g. `floor` on
  `libpcre2-dfa`: 1.897 ns/B on `t-64k-cyr` vs 0.608 ns/B on
  `t-64k-asc`, extract line 183). This is the only R3 population where
  libpcre2 (any route) appears, and the only one where the direction is
  "costs more, not less".

**Verdict:** R3 fires on all three of its own named patterns, on 6 of
the 11 testees, always at the throughput grain the rule restricts itself
to (short-subject `search_short` has no cross-script population for
these three patterns, per NOTES.md's own stated limitation — not
re-derived here, just not violated). The two directions are read as TWO
separate candidate findings (§7): pcrec/vectorscan/rust's `asr-b-ascii`/
`ci-ascii-control` getting CHEAPER off ASCII, and libpcre2's `floor`
(the bare pass-through pattern, `~`) getting MORE expensive on Cyrillic
specifically — `libpcre2-jit` including, which rules out an
interpreter-vs-JIT explanation for that one.

**What was NOT read:** no mechanism stamp is available to distinguish
"a required-byte/lead-byte-frequency effect on the ASCII-vs-non-ASCII
population count" from a corpus-density confound (the `asr-b-ascii`
pattern's boundary condition may simply fire less often per byte on a
script with fewer word/non-word transitions per byte, independent of any
byte-level mechanism) — this reads the numbers only, not the cause, per
R6's own unavailability (§6). `floor`'s three-testee libpcre2 population
(`dfa`/`interp`/`jit`) all sharing the SAME direction and rough magnitude
(3.05-3.12×) across three different match engines argues for a
subject-density explanation over an engine-mechanism one, but this is a
plausibility note, not a scored finding.

## 6. R4 — the script band

> **R4 — the script band.** Within one family, a cell's ns/byte across
> the four per-script 64 KB throughput subjects, spread by more than
> ×4. Four is chosen against the encoded-length ratio the corpora
> themselves carry (1 byte/char for `asc` to ~3 for `cjk`), so anything
> beyond it is not width.

**297 of 750 (pattern, testee) pairs with all 4 subjects present
(39.6%) trip R4** — the largest hit rate of any rule scored in this
addendum. **4 pairs are missing ≥1 of the 4 subjects** and are excluded

| `prp-han` | `rust-default` | asc, cyr, lat (missing cjk) |

extract. This is the ranking discipline working as designed (NOTES.md:
"a cell is listed under the FIRST rule it trips"): these four sit in R0,

**The population is dominated by a mechanical floor, not a script

199-239) are lookbehind assertions (`asr-lb-varwidth` ×920.6 on
`pcrec-vm-in`, `asr-lb-neg` ×408.0, `asr-lb-fixed` ×352.2 — family (e)),

run`, `lit-run-3`, `asr-b-ascii` itself at ×239.8-240.0). In nearly
every worst case, the LOW end of the ratio sits at the SAME floor value
(~0.0168-0.0170 ns/byte, e.g. `asr-lb-varwidth`'s lo=0.017199,
`qnt-lazy-2b`'s lo=0.016912, `asr-lb-neg`'s lo=0.016986) — a "no match
found anywhere in this subject, cheap fast reject" cost shared across
many unrelated patterns, against which ANY subject where the pattern
DOES find work looks enormous by ratio. `alt-cyr-64` (already R5's
universal compile-time-cliff member, §2) appears here too, at the
opposite extreme: ×420.3 (`libpcre2-dfa`) and ×228.0 (`libpcre2-interp`)

itself enormous rather than merely non-floor, consistent with a
64-branch Cyrillic alternation actually doing 64× the comparison work
per matching character on the one script it can match in.

**Verdict:** R4, like R2, reads as closer to a census than an
enumerable finding set at this population size. The ranking rule's own
direction (score the GENERAL-mechanism candidates first) points at the
SAME already-flagged population as O-60's own item 3 (the scan-byte /
required-run finding already in the parent ledger's draft outbox): many
of R4's worst members (`ci-moskva`, `lit-cyr-run`, `lit-1ch-2b`,
`asr-b-ascii`) are literal/required-byte patterns whose necessary byte's
frequency varies sharply by script — the SAME mechanism O-60 item 3
already asks pcrec to confirm, now shown to reproduce as a script-band
outlier under R4's own threshold on many more corpus members than the
seven literals O-60 named.

**What was NOT read:** no mechanism stamp confirms the "fast reject vs.
required-byte-frequency" reading for any INDIVIDUAL R4 hit beyond what

   (`testees/vectorscan/CLAUDE.md`). No new mechanism is proposed; this
   is a census confirming the existing caveat's scale on this set.
3. **`lit-1ch-4b` (the lone 4-byte-character literal, `😀`) is the worst
   R7 high-side member on every pcrec config (ratio 1.95-2.45)** and is
   not otherwise flagged by R2-R5 in this addendum — a candidate for a
   dedicated look, since R7's own charter names exactly this shape
   ("an unanchored ... pattern can go quadratic") as its motivating case,
   though nothing here shows super-linear growth beyond the two-rung
   t-64k/t-1m comparison.
4. **`floor`'s three libpcre2 routes (dfa/interp/jit) all cost ~3.05-
   3.12× more per byte on `t-64k-cyr` than on `t-64k-asc`**, the only R3
   population in the "costs more on non-ASCII" direction and the only
   one where JIT itself is included — worth a look independent of
   pcrec, since `~` (match-anything, the set's own floor pattern) has no
   pcrec-specific mechanism to blame.
5. **R2's and R4's hit rates (32% and 40% of all comparable cells) are
   themselves worth a reading discipline note**: at this scale, neither
   rule is usefully read as "list every hit" — both are closer to R8's
   own census-not-list framing than to R0/R1's per-cell finding shape.
   Whether NOTES.md's ranking clause ("any R2-R7 cell whose likely fix
   is a GENERAL mechanism") should be read as "score the worst members
   only, structurally" for high-hit-rate rules is a question for the
   next set's own outlier-rule design, not a finding about this sample.
